Uptime's July 2026 survey found over half of operators struggling to fill positions.

Equipment refresh cycle · recurring labor over facility life

Beyond the permanent staff, the facility generates recurring labor through the equipment refresh cycle. Virginia’s JLARC found that 68% of a data center’s capital is IT and mechanical equipment, typically replaced about every five years. Each cycle requires on-site installation, cabling, testing, and decommissioning work not captured in the permanent-jobs figure.

Qualitative point, not a headcount estimate.

Brookings (Bahar and Wright) found a county's first large data center lifts total private employment 4-5% over five to six years, and translates that to roughly 2,000-4,000 jobs for a typical county of ~100,000 private jobs. The range shown spans both statements (2-5%). Construction employment rose 11% and information-sector employment 22%, with no significant home-price effect. This captures everything the county gained, including construction-phase activity, so compare it to the tiers rather than adding it.

Colocation: Brookings finds the information-sector gains are driven by hyperscalers. The county range is shown for reference, but a colocation project should expect the low end or below. Brookings also found incentives equal ~62% of total investment for colocation facilities versus ~2% for hyperscale.
